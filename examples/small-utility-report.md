# Example: Small file-renaming utility

> Condensed example. Buyer and price statements are hypotheses until validated.

## Project Snapshot

- **Observed:** A 900-line Go CLI renames media files from templates; no server and no telemetry.
- **Inferred:** Excellent utility, weak standalone SaaS candidate. Repeated agency workflows are more promising than enterprise software.

## What This Project Really Sells

- Fewer repetitive manual edits
- Consistent client deliverables
- Reusable naming rules across batches
- Lower risk of operator mistakes

## Ideal Customer Ranking

| Rank | Customer | Score | Why now |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Media agency handling many client batches | 71 | Time savings repeat and affect delivery margin |
| 2 | Engineering team using it in CI | 67 | Pays for maintenance certainty, not extra commands |
| 3 | Individual power user | 49 | Reachable but low urgency and budget |

## Happiest Sponsor

> I run a small media agency. I would pay for a client-specific batch workflow, saved presets, validation, and setup help. I would not pay a monthly fee just to rename one folder. Give me a repeatable pipeline and I might pay $300–$1,000 for implementation.

## Best Monetization Model

Keep the complete utility free. Start with fixed-scope workflow implementation and optional support. Consider a paid binary bundle only after users repeatedly ask for presets and automatic updates. Do not build hosted infrastructure.

## First $1,000

Interview five agencies in media-production communities. Sell one $500 custom batch workflow and two $250 setup packages. Track which presets repeat before adding product features.

## Build Next

1. Batch validation and dry-run report — Revenue
2. Single-command installation — Adoption
3. Cross-platform CI — Trust
4. Versioned support policy — Enterprise
5. Shareable preset format — Community
