# Competitor Commercial Research

Use this reference to investigate how comparable products attract, convert, charge, retain, and expand customers. The goal is commercial evidence, not a long feature comparison.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Official pricing, checkout, product, docs, terms, licensing, and support pages
2. Official customer stories, partner pages, company blog posts, interviews, and financial filings
3. Reputable reporting, databases, podcasts, conference talks, and founder interviews
4. Review sites, community discussions, job posts, archived pages, and third-party estimates

Use lower-ranked sources as clues, not proof. Search snippets are discovery aids and should not be the sole citation. Record the source URL and publication date; if none exists, record the access date.

For unstable facts such as current prices, plans, ownership, customer counts, revenue, or profitability, verify rather than rely on memory. Seek two independent sources for material financial claims when no first-party filing or statement exists.

## Competitor selection

Build a broad candidate set, then select 3–5 deep dives. Score candidates qualitatively on:

- overlap in paying buyer
- overlap in purchased outcome or job
- relevance of business and distribution model
- currentness and quality of public evidence

Separate:

- **Direct competitor:** similar outcome and buyer
- **Indirect competitor:** same job, different solution
- **Commercial analog:** transferable monetization or sales pattern
- **Status quo:** spreadsheet, internal build, agency, manual work, or doing nothing

Avoid selecting only the most famous companies. A smaller comparable business with public prices and a reachable customer base can be a better analog.

## Research query map

Adapt these queries for each candidate:

- `[company] pricing`, `[product] plans`, `[product] enterprise pricing`
- `[company] customers`, `[company] case study`, `[product] testimonials`
- `[company] revenue`, `[company] profitable`, `[company] annual report`
- `[company] funding`, `[company] acquired`, `[company] bootstrapped`
- `[product] trial`, `[product] demo`, `[product] contact sales`, `[product] checkout`
- `[company] founder interview business model`, `[company] how we grew`
- `[product] reviews`, `[product] alternatives`, `[product] implementation partner`

Search for contradictory evidence, not only confirmation. Open and inspect the actual sources used in the report.

## Competitor dossier

Create one compact dossier per deep competitor:

### Position and buyer

- Product and purchased outcome
- Paying role, user role, and company profile
- Trigger event or painful workaround
- Evidence: customer stories, copy, integrations, reviews, or public contracts

Do not treat website logo walls as proof that most customers share a profile. Describe the sample actually observed and label broader conclusions as inferred.

### Offer and pricing

- Free entry point or lead magnet
- Paid plans and included outcomes
- Pricing unit: seat, usage, workspace, project, success fee, license, support, service, or hybrid
- Public price range and billing period
- Contract or quote-based signals
- Onboarding, implementation, support, and expansion charges when visible

Normalize prices only when the units are genuinely comparable. Preserve original currencies and dates.

### How the sale closes

Reconstruct the observable funnel:

```text
Discovery channel
    → landing promise
    → free tool/content/community/trial/demo
    → activation event
    → checkout or sales conversation
    → onboarding/procurement
    → renewal, usage growth, seats, or upsell
```

Classify the motion:

- **Self-serve:** public price and direct purchase
- **Product-led:** product usage creates or qualifies the sale
- **Founder-led:** founder expertise or direct outreach closes early customers
- **Sales-assisted:** self-serve entry plus human help for larger deals
- **Enterprise sales:** demo/contact sales, security review, procurement, annual contract
- **Services-led:** audit, implementation, customization, training, or support is the transaction
- **Channel-led:** partners, marketplaces, resellers, or ecosystems originate or close business

Infer carefully. A “Book a demo” button supports an enterprise or sales-assisted hypothesis, but does not prove conversion rate, sales-cycle length, or average contract value.

### Financial status

Record these independently:

| Claim | Valid evidence | Invalid shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | filing, founder/company statement, reputable reported figure | funding or web traffic |
| Profitability | explicit net profit, operating profit, cash-flow, or founder statement with period | revenue, bootstrapped status, or layoffs |
| Funding | disclosed round or filing | valuation or investor logo alone |
| Customer scale | dated customer count or disclosed contracts | logo wall alone |

Use one of: **Confirmed**, **Reported**, **Estimated**, or **No reliable public evidence found**. Always attach a period to revenue or profitability claims. Never convert ARR into profit.

### Business-model inference

Express the likely revenue equation:

```text
Revenue ≈ number of paying accounts × paid units per account × price per unit
          + implementation/services + expansion
```

Then identify:

- recurring versus one-time revenue
- likely acquisition mechanism
- primary cost-to-serve driver
- retention or repeat-purchase mechanism
- expansion mechanism
- free-to-paid boundary
- where human labor limits scale

Label this section **Inferred** unless directly documented.

## Cross-competitor synthesis

After the dossiers, compare the competitors on:

- buyer and purchase trigger
- entry offer and acquisition channel
- transaction mechanism and pricing unit
- likely contract size band
- retention and expansion mechanism
- proof of commercial traction
- weaknesses, underserved segments, or costly gaps

Derive monetization paths from repeated patterns and meaningful gaps. A good path states why this project can win despite alternatives: narrower buyer, easier adoption, open-source trust, superior distribution, differentiated data, service expertise, lower operational burden, or a new paid unit.

Do not conclude “the market is validated” merely because competitors exist. Competitors prove that transactions of some kind occur; interviews and payment tests must still prove this project's offer.
