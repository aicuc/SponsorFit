# SponsorFit Scoring

Use scores to compare opportunities, not to manufacture certainty.

## Customer fit score: 100 points

Rate each dimension from 1–5, then multiply by its weight.

| Dimension | Weight | A high score means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Pain intensity | 20 | Frequent, expensive, risky, or blocking problem |
| Willingness to pay | 20 | Budget owner gains measurable value |
| Ease of reaching | 15 | Named communities, users, dependents, or issue authors exist |
| Strategic fit | 15 | Offer reinforces the maintainer's goals and open-source project |
| Product fit | 15 | Existing project solves most of the painful job |
| Revenue potential | 15 | A repeatable offer can reach meaningful contract value |

Calculate each contribution as `rating / 5 × weight`, rounded to a whole point. Show the dimension scores when the ranking is contested; otherwise show the total and weights.

## Interpreting results

- **80–100:** interview immediately; still not validated demand
- **65–79:** credible secondary hypothesis
- **50–64:** explore only if it is especially reachable
- **Below 50:** deprioritize for the current project

Use explicit evidence notes beside ratings. Reduce reachability when the proposed channel is merely a broad social network. Reduce product fit when more than half the paid outcome needs new product development.

## Build opportunity score

For competing features, rate:

- buyer pain (20)
- revenue evidence (20)
- reachability (15)
- technical fit (15)
- competitive advantage (15)
- maintainer effort, reverse-scored (15)

Keep the component ratings visible. If evidence is weak, use qualitative High/Medium/Low instead of a numeric total.
